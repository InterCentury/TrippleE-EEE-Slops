# 13.01 - Essential Electrical and Electronics Formulas

## 1. Ohm’s Law

### Formula

$$V = I \times R$$

### Use Case

Used to calculate voltage, current, or resistance in DC circuits.

```text
V = Voltage (volts, V)
I = Current (amperes, A)
R = Resistance (ohms, Ω)
```

### Rearranged Forms

$$I = \frac{V}{R}$$

$$R = \frac{V}{I}$$

### Meaning

* **Voltage** is the electrical potential difference.
* **Current** is the flow of electric charge.
* **Resistance** is the opposition to current flow.

### Example

```text
V = 12V
R = 6Ω

I = V / R
I = 12 / 6
I = 2A
```

---

## 2. Electrical Power

### Formula

$$P = V \times I$$

### Use Case

Used to calculate how much electrical power a circuit consumes or delivers.

```text
P = Power (watts, W)
V = Voltage (volts, V)
I = Current (amperes, A)
```

### Rearranged Forms

$$V = \frac{P}{I}$$

$$I = \frac{P}{V}$$

### Meaning

* **Power** is the rate of energy transfer.
* Higher power means more energy is used per second.

### Example

```text
V = 12V
I = 2A

P = V × I
P = 12 × 2
P = 24W
```

---

## 3. Power Dissipation in a Resistor

### Formula

$$P = I^2R$$

### Use Case

Used to calculate heat generated in resistors, wires, PCB traces, and MOSFETs.

```text
P = Power dissipated (W)
I = Current (A)
R = Resistance (Ω)
```

### Meaning

* The heating effect grows very quickly as current increases.
* Doubling current makes power four times larger.

### Example

```text
I = 3A
R = 2Ω

P = I²R
P = 3² × 2
P = 9 × 2
P = 18W
```

---

## 4. Power From Voltage and Resistance

### Formula

$$P = \frac{V^2}{R}$$

### Use Case

Used when voltage and resistance are known.

```text
P = Power (W)
V = Voltage (V)
R = Resistance (Ω)
```

### Meaning

* More voltage across a fixed resistance creates more power loss.
* Useful for checking resistor heating.

### Example

```text
V = 10V
R = 5Ω

P = V² / R
P = 100 / 5
P = 20W
```

---

## 5. Series Resistance

### Formula

$$R_{total} = R_1 + R_2 + R_3 + \cdots$$

### Use Case

Used when resistors are connected end-to-end in series.

```text
Rtotal = total resistance (Ω)
R1, R2, R3 = individual resistances (Ω)
```

### Meaning

* Series resistances add directly.
* Total resistance becomes larger.

### Example

```text
R1 = 10Ω
R2 = 20Ω
R3 = 30Ω

Rtotal = 10 + 20 + 30
Rtotal = 60Ω
```

---

## 6. Parallel Resistance

### Formula

$$\frac{1}{R_{total}} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} + \frac{1}{R_3} + \cdots$$

### Use Case

Used when resistors are connected side by side in parallel.

```text
Rtotal = total resistance (Ω)
R1, R2, R3 = branch resistances (Ω)
```

### Meaning

* Parallel resistance becomes smaller than the smallest branch resistance.
* Current splits among branches.

### Example

```text
R1 = 10Ω
R2 = 10Ω

1/Rtotal = 1/10 + 1/10
1/Rtotal = 2/10
Rtotal = 5Ω
```

---

## 7. Current Divider

### Formula

$$I_1 = I_{total} \times \frac{R_2}{R_1 + R_2}$$

$$I_2 = I_{total} \times \frac{R_1}{R_1 + R_2}$$

### Use Case

Used to find how current divides in a parallel circuit.

```text
I1 = current through branch 1 (A)
I2 = current through branch 2 (A)
Itotal = total current entering the parallel network (A)
R1, R2 = branch resistances (Ω)
```

### Meaning

* Lower resistance branch gets more current.
* Higher resistance branch gets less current.

---

## 8. Voltage Divider

### Formula

$$V_{out} = V_{in} \times \frac{R_2}{R_1 + R_2}$$

### Use Case

Used to reduce voltage to a lower value.

```text
Vin = input voltage (V)
Vout = output voltage (V)
R1, R2 = divider resistors (Ω)
```

### Meaning

* The output voltage is a fraction of the input voltage.
* Used in analog sensing and reference circuits.

### Example

```text
Vin = 12V
R1 = 3kΩ
R2 = 1kΩ

Vout = 12 × 1000 / (3000 + 1000)
Vout = 12 × 1/4
Vout = 3V
```

---

## 9. Charge

### Formula

$$Q = I \times t$$

### Use Case

Used to calculate how much electric charge flows over time.

```text
Q = Charge (coulombs, C)
I = Current (A)
t = Time (seconds, s)
```

### Meaning

* Current is charge per second.
* More time or more current means more total charge.

### Example

```text
I = 2A
t = 5s

Q = 2 × 5
Q = 10C
```

---

## 10. Energy

### Formula

$$E = P \times t$$

### Use Case

Used to calculate total energy used over a period of time.

```text
E = Energy (joules, J)
P = Power (W)
t = Time (s)
```

### Meaning

* Energy is the total work done over time.
* More time at the same power means more energy.

### Example

```text
P = 20W
t = 10s

E = 20 × 10
E = 200J
```

---

## 11. Capacitor Charge

### Formula

$$Q = C \times V$$

### Use Case

Used to determine how much charge a capacitor stores.

```text
Q = Charge (C)
C = Capacitance (farads, F)
V = Voltage (V)
```

### Meaning

* Larger capacitance stores more charge at the same voltage.
* Higher voltage stores more charge in the same capacitor.

### Example

```text
C = 1000µF = 0.001F
V = 5V

Q = 0.001 × 5
Q = 0.005C
```

---

## 12. Capacitor Current-Voltage Relation

### Formula

$$I = C \frac{dV}{dt}$$

### Use Case

Used to describe how capacitor current depends on how fast voltage changes.

```text
I = Current (A)
C = Capacitance (F)
dV/dt = rate of voltage change (V/s)
```

### Meaning

* Fast voltage change causes more current.
* Capacitors resist sudden voltage changes.

---

## 13. Capacitor Time Constant in RC Circuits

### Formula

$$\tau = R \times C$$

### Use Case

Used in charging and discharging timing circuits.

```text
τ = time constant (seconds)
R = resistance (Ω)
C = capacitance (F)
```

### Meaning

* Larger R or C means slower charging/discharging.
* After one time constant, the voltage changes significantly.

### Example

```text
R = 10kΩ = 10000Ω
C = 100µF = 0.0001F

τ = 10000 × 0.0001
τ = 1s
```

---

## 14. Capacitor Charging Voltage

### Formula

$$V_C(t) = V_{s}\left(1 - e^{-t/RC}\right)$$

### Use Case

Used to calculate capacitor voltage while charging.

```text
VC(t) = capacitor voltage at time t
Vs = supply voltage (V)
t = time (s)
R = resistance (Ω)
C = capacitance (F)
e = Euler’s number
```

### Meaning

* The capacitor voltage rises gradually.
* It never jumps instantly to the final voltage.

---

## 15. Capacitor Discharging Voltage

### Formula

$$V_C(t) = V_0 e^{-t/RC}$$

### Use Case

Used to calculate capacitor voltage while discharging.

```text
VC(t) = capacitor voltage at time t
V0 = initial capacitor voltage (V)
t = time (s)
R = resistance (Ω)
C = capacitance (F)
```

### Meaning

* The capacitor voltage falls exponentially.
* Larger RC means slower discharge.

---

## 16. Inductive Voltage

### Formula

$$V_L = L \frac{di}{dt}$$

### Use Case

Used to calculate voltage created by a changing current in an inductor.

```text
VL = inductor voltage (V)
L = inductance (henry, H)
di/dt = rate of current change (A/s)
```

### Meaning

* Inductors resist sudden current changes.
* Fast current change produces large voltage spikes.

### Example

```text
L = 2H
di/dt = 3A/s

VL = 2 × 3
VL = 6V
```

---

## 17. Inductive Energy Storage

### Formula

$$E = \frac{1}{2}LI^2$$

### Use Case

Used to determine energy stored in an inductor.

```text
E = Energy (J)
L = inductance (H)
I = current (A)
```

### Meaning

* Energy stored in an inductor grows with the square of current.
* High current in coils can store a lot of energy.

---

## 18. Frequency and Period

### Formula

$$f = \frac{1}{T}$$

$$T = \frac{1}{f}$$

### Use Case

Used to convert between frequency and period.

```text
f = frequency (Hz)
T = period (seconds)
```

### Meaning

* Frequency is how many cycles happen per second.
* Period is the time for one full cycle.

### Example

```text
f = 50Hz

T = 1/50
T = 0.02s
```

---

## 19. Angular Frequency

### Formula

$$\omega = 2\pi f$$

### Use Case

Used in AC analysis, filters, and sinusoidal signal equations.

```text
ω = angular frequency (rad/s)
f = frequency (Hz)
π = pi
```

### Meaning

* Converts ordinary frequency into radians per second.
* Useful in AC mathematics.

---

## 20. AC RMS Voltage

### Formula

$$V_{rms} = \frac{V_{peak}}{\sqrt{2}}$$

### Use Case

Used to find the effective voltage of a sine wave.

```text
Vrms = root mean square voltage (V)
Vpeak = peak voltage (V)
```

### Meaning

* RMS voltage is the equivalent DC heating value.
* Household AC values are usually RMS values.

### Example

```text
Vpeak = 20V

Vrms = 20 / √2
Vrms ≈ 14.14V
```

---

## 21. AC RMS Current

### Formula

$$I_{rms} = \frac{I_{peak}}{\sqrt{2}}$$

### Use Case

Used to find the effective current of a sine wave.

```text
Irms = root mean square current (A)
Ipeak = peak current (A)
```

---

## 22. Impedance of a Capacitor

### Formula

$$X_C = \frac{1}{2\pi f C}$$

### Use Case

Used to find how much a capacitor resists AC current.

```text
XC = capacitive reactance (Ω)
f = frequency (Hz)
C = capacitance (F)
```

### Meaning

* Higher frequency means lower capacitor reactance.
* Larger capacitance means lower reactance.

---

## 23. Impedance of an Inductor

### Formula

$$X_L = 2\pi f L$$

### Use Case

Used to find how much an inductor resists AC current.

```text
XL = inductive reactance (Ω)
f = frequency (Hz)
L = inductance (H)
```

### Meaning

* Higher frequency means higher inductor reactance.
* Larger inductance means higher reactance.

---

## 24. RC Low-Pass Cutoff Frequency

### Formula

$$f_c = \frac{1}{2\pi RC}$$

### Use Case

Used in filters to determine cutoff frequency.

```text
fc = cutoff frequency (Hz)
R = resistance (Ω)
C = capacitance (F)
```

### Meaning

* Frequencies below cutoff pass more easily.
* Frequencies above cutoff are reduced.

---

## 25. RL Time Constant

### Formula

$$\tau = \frac{L}{R}$$

### Use Case

Used in inductive circuits to describe current rise and fall speed.

```text
τ = time constant (seconds)
L = inductance (H)
R = resistance (Ω)
```

### Meaning

* Larger inductance slows current change.
* Larger resistance makes current settle faster.

---

## 26. Inductor Current Growth

### Formula

$$i(t) = I_{final}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right)$$

### Use Case

Used to describe current rise in an RL circuit.

```text
i(t) = current at time t
Ifinal = final steady current (A)
t = time (s)
τ = RL time constant
```

---

## 27. Resonant Frequency

### Formula

$$f_0 = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}}$$

### Use Case

Used in LC filters and resonant circuits.

```text
f0 = resonant frequency (Hz)
L = inductance (H)
C = capacitance (F)
```

### Meaning

* At resonance, inductive and capacitive reactance balance each other.
* Common in tuning and oscillator circuits.

---

## 28. Diode Equation

### Formula

$$I = I_S\left(e^{\frac{V_D}{nV_T}} - 1\right)$$

### Use Case

Used to describe diode current behavior more accurately.

```text
I = diode current (A)
IS = saturation current (A)
VD = diode voltage (V)
n = ideality factor
VT = thermal voltage (V)
```

### Meaning

* Diodes do not behave like perfect switches.
* Current rises exponentially after forward bias.

---

## 29. Thermal Voltage

### Formula

$$V_T = \frac{kT}{q}$$

### Use Case

Used in semiconductor physics.

```text
VT = thermal voltage (V)
k = Boltzmann constant
T = temperature in kelvin (K)
q = electron charge
```

### Meaning

* Thermal voltage changes with temperature.
* It is important in diode and transistor equations.

---

## 30. Junction Temperature Rise

### Formula

$$\Delta T = P \times R_{\theta}$$

### Use Case

Used to estimate temperature rise in electronic parts.

```text
ΔT = temperature rise (°C)
P = power dissipated (W)
Rθ = thermal resistance (°C/W)
```

### Meaning

* More power means more heat.
* Higher thermal resistance means worse cooling.

---

## 31. Thermal Power in a Regulator

### Formula

$$P = (V_{in} - V_{out}) \times I$$

### Use Case

Used for linear regulator heat loss.

```text
Vin = input voltage (V)
Vout = output voltage (V)
I = load current (A)
```

### Meaning

* Extra voltage is converted into heat.
* Important for regulator heatsink design.

### Example

```text
Vin = 12V
Vout = 5V
I = 1A

P = (12 - 5) × 1
P = 7W
```

---

```text
E = energy (Wh, if voltage is in V and Ah is ampere-hour)

```

## 34. Efficiency

### Formula

$$\eta = \frac{P_{out}}{P_{in}} \times 100%$$

### Use Case

Used to calculate how efficiently a circuit converts input power into useful output power.

```text
η = efficiency
Pout = output power
Pin = input power
```

### Meaning

* Higher efficiency means less wasted energy as heat.

---

## 35. Kirchhoff’s Current Law

### Formula

$$\sum I_{in} = \sum I_{out}$$

### Use Case

Used to analyze current at a node.

```text
Current entering a node = current leaving a node
```

### Meaning

* Current does not disappear.
* What enters a node must leave the node.

---

## 36. Kirchhoff’s Voltage Law

### Formula

$$\sum V = 0$$

### Use Case

Used to analyze voltage around a closed loop.

```text
Sum of voltage rises and drops in a loop = 0
```

### Meaning

* Energy gained and lost around a loop must balance.

---

## 37. Transformer Turns Ratio

### Formula

$$\frac{V_p}{V_s} = \frac{N_p}{N_s}$$

### Use Case

Used in transformers to relate primary and secondary voltage.

```text
Vp = primary voltage
Vs = secondary voltage
Np = primary turns
Ns = secondary turns
```

### Meaning

* Voltage ratio matches turns ratio in an ideal transformer.

---

## 38. Transformer Current Ratio

### Formula

$$\frac{I_p}{I_s} = \frac{N_s}{N_p}$$

### Use Case

Used to relate current on primary and secondary sides.

### Meaning

* Higher voltage side usually has lower current, and vice versa.

---

## 39. BJT Current Gain

### Formula

$$I_C = \beta I_B$$

### Use Case

Used to estimate transistor collector current.

```text
IC = collector current (A)
β = current gain
IB = base current (A)
```

### Meaning

* A small base current can control a larger collector current.
* Real BJTs need proper base drive to saturate correctly.

---

## 40. MOSFET Conduction Loss

### Formula

$$P = I^2R_{DS(on)}$$

### Use Case

Used to estimate MOSFET heating when it is fully on.

```text
P = power loss (W)
I = current (A)
RDS(on) = on-state resistance (Ω)
```

### Meaning

* Lower RDS(on) means less heat.
* Important for motor drivers and power switching.

---

## 41. Data Rate and Bandwidth Relation

### Formula

$$T_b = \frac{1}{R_b}$$

### Use Case

Used in digital communications.

```text
Tb = bit time (s)
Rb = bit rate (bits/s)
```

### Meaning

* Faster data rate means shorter time per bit.

---

## 42. Sampling Nyquist Rule

### Formula

$$f_s \ge 2f_{max}$$

### Use Case

Used in signal sampling and ADC theory.

```text
fs = sample rate
fmax = highest signal frequency
```

### Meaning

* Sampling must be at least twice the highest signal frequency to avoid aliasing.

---

## 43. ADC Resolution Step Size

### Formula

$$\text{LSB} = \frac{V_{ref}}{2^n}$$

### Use Case

Used to calculate ADC voltage resolution.

```text
LSB = smallest voltage step
Vref = reference voltage
n = number of ADC bits
```

### Meaning

* More bits mean finer measurement steps.

## 45. Quick Use Guide

* **Need current?** Use Ohm’s law: $$I=\frac{V}{R}$$
* **Need heat?** Use power formulas: $$P=VI$$, $$P=I^2R$$
* **Need divider output?** Use: $$V_{out}=V_{in}\frac{R_2}{R_1+R_2}$$
* **Need capacitor timing?** Use: $$\tau=RC$$
* **Need inductor behavior?** Use: $$V=L\frac{di}{dt}$$
* **Need AC equivalent?** Use RMS formulas and reactance formulas
* **Need thermal estimate?** Use: $$\Delta T=P\times R_{\theta}$$
