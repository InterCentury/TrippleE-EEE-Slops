# 13.03 - Electrical and Electronics Units: Meanings, Relationships, and Formula Connections

## 1. Why Units Matter

Units are the language of measurement in electronics. A formula is only useful when every symbol has a clear physical meaning and a correct unit.

A wrong unit can create a wrong design even if the algebra is correct.

```text
Number alone = incomplete
Number + unit = physical meaning
```

For example:

* `5` is only a number
* `5V` means electrical potential difference
* `5A` means current
* `5W` means power

These are not interchangeable.

```text id="u1q2w3"
> UNIT CONNECTION IDEA

   Voltage, current, resistance, power,
   charge, capacitance, inductance, and frequency
   are all connected through circuit laws.
```

## 2. Base Electrical Units

### 2.1 Volt

**Symbol:** `V`
**Unit name:** volt
**What it indicates:** electrical potential difference, or electrical “pressure”

### Meaning

Voltage tells how strongly a circuit can push charge through a path.

### Relationship

$$1V = 1 \frac{J}{C}$$

That means one volt is one joule of energy per coulomb of charge.

```text id="v4r5t6"
> VOLT CONNECTION

   Voltage = Energy per Charge
   V = J / C
```

---

### 2.2 Ampere

**Symbol:** `A`
**Unit name:** ampere
**What it indicates:** electric current, or flow of charge

### Meaning

Current tells how much charge passes a point each second.

### Relationship

$$1A = 1 \frac{C}{s}$$

That means one ampere is one coulomb per second.

```text id="a7s8d9"
> AMPERE CONNECTION

   Current = Charge per Second
   I = C / s
```

---

### 2.3 Ohm

**Symbol:** `Ω`
**Unit name:** ohm
**What it indicates:** resistance

### Meaning

Resistance tells how strongly a material opposes current flow.

### Relationship

$$1\Omega = 1 \frac{V}{A}$$

That means one ohm is one volt per ampere.

```text id="o1p2a3"
> OHM CONNECTION

   Resistance = Voltage / Current
   R = V / I
```

---

### 2.4 Watt

**Symbol:** `W`
**Unit name:** watt
**What it indicates:** power

### Meaning

Power is the rate at which energy is transferred or consumed.

### Relationships

$$1W = 1 \frac{J}{s}$$

and also

$$P = V \times I$$

```text id="w4e5r6"
> WATT CONNECTION

   Power = Voltage × Current
   P = V × I
```

---

### 2.5 Coulomb

**Symbol:** `C`
**Unit name:** coulomb
**What it indicates:** electric charge

### Meaning

Charge is the total quantity of electricity.

### Relationship

$$1C = 1A \times 1s$$

```text id="c7t8y9"
> COULOMB CONNECTION

   Charge = Current × Time
   Q = I × t
```

---

### 2.6 Farad

**Symbol:** `F`
**Unit name:** farad
**What it indicates:** capacitance

### Meaning

Capacitance tells how much charge a capacitor stores per volt.

### Relationship

$$1F = 1 \frac{C}{V}$$

```text id="f1g2h3"
> FARAD CONNECTION

   Capacitance = Charge / Voltage
   C = Q / V
```

---

### 2.7 Henry

**Symbol:** `H`
**Unit name:** henry
**What it indicates:** inductance

### Meaning

Inductance tells how strongly a coil resists changes in current.

### Relationship

$$1H = 1 \frac{V \cdot s}{A}$$

```text id="h4j5k6"
> HENRY CONNECTION

   Inductance = Voltage × Time / Current
```

---

### 2.8 Hertz

**Symbol:** `Hz`
**Unit name:** hertz
**What it indicates:** frequency

### Meaning

Frequency is the number of cycles per second.

### Relationship

$$1Hz = 1 \frac{1}{s}$$

```text id="hz7l8m"
> HERTZ CONNECTION

   Frequency = 1 / Period
   f = 1 / T
```

---

### 2.9 Joule

**Symbol:** `J`
**Unit name:** joule
**What it indicates:** energy

### Meaning

Energy is the total work done or stored.

### Relationships

$$1J = 1W \cdot s$$

and also

$$1J = 1V \cdot C$$

```text id="j9n0o1"
> JOULE CONNECTION

   Energy = Power × Time
   E = P × t
```

---

### 2.10 Siemens

**Symbol:** `S`
**Unit name:** siemens
**What it indicates:** conductance

### Meaning

Conductance is the inverse of resistance.

### Relationship

$$1S = 1 \frac{A}{V} = \frac{1}{\Omega}$$

```text id="s2d3f4"
> SIEMENS CONNECTION

   Conductance = 1 / Resistance
   G = 1 / R
```

## 3. Core Formula Connections Between Units

These are the most important relationships in electronics.

### 3.1 Ohm’s Law Connection

$$V = I \times R$$

From this:

$$I = \frac{V}{R}$$

$$R = \frac{V}{I}$$

### Unit connection

* volts connect to amps through ohms
* resistance determines how much current flows for a given voltage

```text id="c5v6b7"
> OHM'S LAW UNIT MAP

   V = I × R
   A × Ω = V
   V / Ω = A
   V / A = Ω
```

---

### 3.2 Power Connection

$$P = V \times I$$

Using Ohm’s Law:

$$P = I^2R$$

and

$$P = \frac{V^2}{R}$$

### Unit connection

* volts and amps combine to make watts
* watts measure heat production or useful work rate

```text id="p8n9m0"
> POWER UNIT MAP

   W = V × A
   W = A² × Ω
   W = V² / Ω
```

---

### 3.3 Charge Connection

$$Q = I \times t$$

### Unit connection

* amperes times seconds give coulombs
* charge is accumulated current over time

```text id="q1w2e3"
> CHARGE UNIT MAP

   C = A × s
```

---

### 3.4 Energy Connection

$$E = P \times t$$

and since

$$P = V \times I$$

then:

$$E = V \times I \times t$$

### Unit connection

* joules connect to watts and seconds
* energy tells how much total work is done

```text id="e4r5t6"
> ENERGY UNIT MAP

   J = W × s
   J = V × A × s
   J = V × C
```

---

### 3.5 Capacitance Connection

$$C = \frac{Q}{V}$$

### Unit connection

* farads connect charge and voltage
* a larger capacitance stores more charge at the same voltage

```text id="f7g8h9"
> CAPACITANCE UNIT MAP

   F = C / V
```

---

### 3.6 Inductance Connection

$$V = L \frac{di}{dt}$$

### Unit connection

* henries connect voltage and changing current
* inductors oppose sudden current change

```text id="l0k1j2"
> INDUCTANCE UNIT MAP

   H = V × s / A
```

## 5. Electrical Units and Their Links

### 5.1 Voltage and Current

Voltage pushes current.

```text
V leads to I through R
```

### 5.2 Current and Resistance

Higher resistance means lower current for the same voltage.

### 5.3 Current and Power

More current usually means more power dissipation in real components.

### 5.4 Voltage and Power

Higher voltage across a fixed resistance increases power strongly.

### 5.5 Charge and Current

Current is charge moving per second.

### 5.6 Energy and Power

Power is energy per second.

```text id="u4i5o6"
> UNIT FLOW CHAIN

   Charge → Current → Voltage → Power → Energy
```

This is not a one-way chain in formulas only; these units are all mathematically connected and often converted back and forth during circuit analysis.

## 7. Thermal and Physical Units Often Used in Electronics

### 7.1 Degree Celsius

**Symbol:** `°C`
**Meaning:** temperature on the Celsius scale

### 7.2 Kelvin

**Symbol:** `K`
**Meaning:** absolute temperature scale

### 7.3 Thermal Resistance

**Symbol:** `Rθ`
**Unit:** `°C/W`
**Meaning:** temperature rise per watt of dissipation

### Relationship

$$\Delta T = P \times R_{\theta}$$

```text id="t4r5y6"
> THERMAL UNIT CONNECTION

   watts of heat × thermal resistance = temperature rise
```

## 8. Frequency Domain Unit Relationships

### 8.1 Period and Frequency

$$f = \frac{1}{T}$$

Where:

* `f` is in hertz
* `T` is in seconds

### 8.2 Angular Frequency

$$\omega = 2\pi f$$

Where:

* `ω` is in radians per second
* `f` is in hertz

### 8.3 Reactance Units

Capacitive reactance and inductive reactance are both measured in ohms.

$$X_C = \frac{1}{2\pi f C}$$

$$X_L = 2\pi f L$$

So:

* `X_C` is in ohms
* `X_L` is in ohms

```text id="f8g9h0"
> FREQUENCY UNIT RELATION

   Hz → ω → X_C / X_L → Ω
```

## 9. Semiconductor-Related Units

### 9.1 Diode Voltage Drop

**Symbol:** `Vf`
**Meaning:** forward voltage of a diode

### 9.2 Reverse Voltage

**Symbol:** `Vr`
**Meaning:** maximum reverse blocking voltage

### 9.3 Current Gain

**Symbol:** `β` or `hFE`
**Meaning:** transistor current gain, not a unit, but commonly paired with current units

### 9.4 Conductance and Resistance

A transistor channel may be described by resistance or conductance values.

```text id="m1n2o3"
> SEMICONDUCTOR UNIT LINK

   voltage and current define device behavior
   but heat and resistance define survival
```

## 10. Measurement Units in Datasheets

Datasheets use units constantly to define safe operation.

### Common examples

* `VCC` in volts
* `IC` in amperes
* `PD` in watts
* `TJ` in degrees Celsius
* `RDS(on)` in ohms
* `ESR` in ohms
* `fSW` in hertz
* `QG` in coulombs
* `C` in farads
* `L` in henries

```text id="r7s8t9"
> DATASHEET CONNECTIONS

   voltage ratings → volts
   current limits → amps
   losses → watts
   timing → seconds / hertz
   storage → coulombs / farads / henries
```

## 11. Practical Electronics Unit Map

```text id="k2l3m4"
> UNIT NETWORK

   V (voltage)
     │
     ├── I = V / R
     ├── P = V × I
     ├── E = V × Q
     └── W = V² / R

   I (current)
     │
     ├── Q = I × t
     ├── P = I²R
     └── V = I × R

   R (resistance)
     │
     ├── limits current
     ├── creates heat
     └── defines voltage division

   C (capacitance)
     │
     ├── Q = CV
     ├── τ = RC
     └── Xc = 1 / (2πfC)

   L (inductance)
     │
     ├── VL = L(di/dt)
     ├── τ = L/R
     └── Xl = 2πfL
```

## 12. Quick Symbol-to-Meaning Reference

1. **V**: voltage, electrical potential difference
2. **I**: current, flow of electric charge
3. **R**: resistance, opposition to current
4. **P**: power, rate of energy transfer
5. **E**: energy, total work or stored energy
6. **Q**: charge, quantity of electricity
7. **C**: capacitance or coulomb depending on context
8. **L**: inductance
9. **f**: frequency
10. **T**: period
11. **ω**: angular frequency
12. **Ω**: ohm, resistance unit
13. **F**: farad, capacitance unit
14. **H**: henry, inductance unit
15. **Hz**: hertz, frequency unit
16. **J**: joule, energy unit
17. **W**: watt, power unit
18. **S**: siemens, conductance unit
19. **dB**: decibel, ratio unit
20. **°C**: degree Celsius, temperature
21. **K**: kelvin, absolute temperature

## 13. Final Unit Logic

If you understand these unit links, you can move through almost any electronics formula:

* voltage and current give power
* current and resistance give heat
* current and time give charge
* charge and voltage give capacitance
* changing current and inductance give voltage spikes
* power and thermal resistance give temperature rise
* frequency and capacitance/inductance determine AC behavior

That is the core map of EEE units.
